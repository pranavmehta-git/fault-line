📐 Methodology

Overview

The Fault Line provides a transparent, evidence-based assessment of structural vulnerabilities facing frontier AI laboratories. Rather than predicting outcomes, it monitors and quantifies dependencies and risk factors that could affect an organization's ability to operate, scale, or adapt.

Each lab receives a Fragility Score from 0–10, where higher scores indicate greater systemic fragility. Scores accumulate continuously from publicly verifiable news events; every claim in the score traces back to a dated, linked source.

Scoring Formula (v3)

Earlier versions used a binary checklist: any single event inside a 180-day window permanently "lit" an indicator, and the score was the count of lit indicators. That design saturated — heavily covered labs pinned at 10/10 and could not register further deterioration — and it conflated media volume with fragility. Version 3 replaces the binary trigger with a continuous evidence model:

event signal = confidence weight × source-type weight × time decay
  • Confidence: high = 1.0×, medium = 0.6×, low = 0.3×
  • Source type: regulatory filings = 1.25×, news = 1.0×, self-published lab blogs = 0.5×
  • Time decay: exponential with a 90-day half-life (no hard cutoff cliff); events beyond 365 days contribute nothing

Corroboration, not volume. For each checklist indicator, signals are grouped by publisher domain and only the strongest signal per domain counts. Ten articles from one outlet are one piece of evidence; the same story confirmed by three independent outlets is three. Total evidence E maps to an indicator intensity through a saturating curve:

intensity = E / (E + 1)   ∈ [0, 1)

One fresh, high-confidence report yields intensity 0.5; three independent confirmations yield 0.75; ten yield 0.91. Additional evidence always moves the score (no frozen ceiling) but with diminishing returns (no runaway volume bias). Events whose impact direction contradicts an indicator — e.g. good-news coverage that merely mentions a risk keyword — are excluded.

The lab total is then:

Total = 10 × max(0, Compute + Cloud + Policy + Demand + Societal Impact + Talent & Governance − Resilience) / 12

Each dimension contributes 0–2 continuous points, except Resilience which reduces the total. A score of 10 is an asymptote — reachable only by broad, corroborated, recent stress across every dimension with no demonstrated mitigation — never a resting state.

Systemic Risk Index

Lab-level scores treat each lab as independent, but the defining property of frontier-AI risk is that it is correlated: the labs share suppliers, cloud platforms, regulatory environments, and capital cycles. The Systemic Risk Index (0–10) measures industry-wide, common-mode risk from four components:

  • Average lab fragility (35%): the mean fragility score across labs — the level term.
  • Correlated stress (30%): the share of labs simultaneously stressed on the same dimension. One lab with compute problems is idiosyncratic; four of five at once is a sector-wide failure mode.
  • Shared dependency (20%): concentration of all labs on the same few suppliers (NVIDIA, Azure, AWS, Google Cloud/TPU), measured from supplier mentions in recent events. A single supplier shock propagates to every exposed lab at once.
  • Score co-movement (15%): the trailing 12-month correlation of lab score histories. Labs whose fragility rises and falls together are responding to common shocks, not lab-specific ones.

Each component is reported separately in scores.json so the index is fully decomposable.

Dimensions Explained

💾 Compute & Chips Dependence (0–2 pts)

Measures reliance on specific hardware vendors and exposure to supply chain disruptions. Labs with single-vendor GPU strategies or documented supply constraints score higher.

☁️ Cloud Concentration (0–2 pts)

Measures dependency on hyperscaler partnerships for training and inference infrastructure. Exclusive partnerships, deep integrations, and high switching costs contribute to fragility.

🏛️ Policy & Geopolitical Exposure (0–2 pts)

Measures sensitivity to regulatory action, export controls, and political shifts. Labs operating across jurisdictions with pending regulations or active investigations score higher.

📈 Demand & Commercialization (0–2 pts)

Measures revenue sustainability and market position risks. Signals of demand weakness, customer churn, or capex/opex overhang relative to adoption increase this score.

🛡️ Resilience Moves (0–2 pts, inverted)

Measures proactive risk mitigation. Multi-sourcing strategies, diversified infrastructure, long-term contracts, and demonstrated redundancy reduce the total fragility score.

🌍 Societal Impact (0–2 pts)

Measures signals of workforce displacement, misinformation amplification, privacy/surveillance concerns, power concentration, and safety incidents attributed to AI deployment.

👥 Talent & Governance (0–2 pts)

Measures leadership instability, key departures, board dysfunction, and organizational governance risks. The historical record contains 15+ high-impact events of this type — the Altman firing, Leike departure, Sutskever exit, and similar episodes — that the original five-dimension checklist could not score.

Evidence Requirements

Every contribution to a score must be supported by:

  • A verifiable news event with a source URL and date
  • A confidence rating (low/medium/high) based on classification strength and source reliability
  • An impact direction consistent with the indicator — favorable coverage cannot add fragility

Evidence decays exponentially (90-day half-life), so indicators fade gradually unless reaffirmed by new events; nothing expires on a cliff.

Contested Items

If contradictory evidence exists for a checklist item (e.g., both signals of lock-in and diversification), the item is marked as "contested" and requires manual review before affecting the score.

Contested items are displayed in the UI with both supporting and contradicting evidence visible.

Data Sources

Fault Line ingests from multiple source tiers to catch signals at different stages:

  • Tier 1 — Regulatory & Institutional: SEC EDGAR company filings (Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta), FTC press releases, DOJ press releases, EU AI Office publications. These catch regulatory signals before they reach mainstream news.
  • Tier 1 — Lab Blogs: Official blogs from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Meta AI.
  • Tier 1–2 — Tech & Science News: TechCrunch, VentureBeat, Reuters, MIT Technology Review, Nature, Ars Technica, The Verge, Wired, and Science Magazine.

Data Pipeline

The tracker updates automatically via the following process:

  1. Ingestion: RSS feeds and curated sources are checked for new articles
  2. Classification: Articles are mapped to labs, dimensions, and checklist items
  3. Deduplication: Duplicate events are detected and merged
  4. Scoring: Checklist states are updated and scores recalculated
  5. Publication: JSON data files are committed to the repository

The pipeline runs daily via GitHub Actions. Manual event submissions are accepted via GitHub Issues.

Limitations & Caveats

This tracker has important limitations:

  • Public information only: Private deals, internal metrics, and unreported events are not captured
  • Coverage asymmetry: Labs that receive less press coverage (or publish no RSS feed, like xAI) accumulate less evidence, which biases their scores downward. Corroboration weighting and per-lab coverage reporting reduce this bias but cannot eliminate it — a low score can mean low fragility or thin coverage
  • Keyword classification: Events are classified by keyword matching, which misses context and severity; confidence ratings only partially compensate
  • Lag: News may lag actual events by days or weeks
  • Selection bias: Source selection affects what events are captured
  • Not predictive: Fragility scores measure exposure, not likelihood of negative outcomes

Users should treat scores as a starting point for analysis, not definitive assessments.

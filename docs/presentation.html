01 / THE PROBLEM

Governance monitors capabilities and incidents—but not organizational fragility.

  • AI labs are becoming critical infrastructure, yet their structural dependencies remain untracked
  • Public signals—compute lock-in, leadership churn, policy exposure—are visible before crises materialize
  • No lightweight, transparent tool exists to map these risk factors systematically

02 / THE APPROACH

Public evidence, structured into an auditable fragility monitor.

  • Tracks 7 dimensions of organizational risk using publicly verifiable events
  • Scores via a transparent checklist—every point traceable to a source
  • Designed as decision support, not prediction: an instrument panel, not a crystal ball

03 / METHOD

Ingest. Classify. Score. Expose.

  • Ingest news, filings, and official signals from public sources
  • Map each event to one of 7 fragility dimensions with a confidence tag
  • Score labs via binary checklist + confidence-weighted variant
  • Expose evidence trails, trajectories, and per-lab breakdowns in an open dashboard

04 / USE CASES

Shared situational awareness for the AI governance ecosystem.

  • Researchers — a scaffold for empirical work on AI political economy
  • Policymakers — a quick read on concentration, dependency, and pressure points
  • Journalists — a structured lead generator for weak signals before crises
  • Labs themselves — a mirror for resilience gaps and governance debt

05 / LIMITATIONS

Transparent about what it cannot do—yet.

  • Selection bias — public reporting overweights visible, English-language firms
  • Subjective coding — even transparent heuristics embed judgment calls
  • No causal guarantee — fragility indicators are warning signs, not deterministic forecasts

A transparent, imperfect monitor still outperforms ad hoc intuition—especially when its limitations are visible and its evidence is open.

If frontier labs are becoming systemically important institutions, governance should have better dashboards for their fragility.

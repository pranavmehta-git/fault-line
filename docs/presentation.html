01 / THE PROBLEM

Governance monitors capabilities and incidents, but nobody tracks the structural fragility of AI labs themselves.

✓
CapabilitiesBenchmarks, evals, model cards
✓
Safety practiceCommitments, audits, red-teaming
⚠
Structural fragilityDependencies, lock-in, governance debt
✓
Incident responsePost-hoc harm cataloging
Speaker Notes:
  • AI labs are becoming critical infrastructure, yet their structural dependencies remain untracked.
  • Public signals like compute lock-in, leadership churn, and policy exposure are visible before crises materialize.
  • No lightweight, transparent tool exists to map these risk factors systematically.

  • Scores via a transparent checklist — every point traceable to a source.
  • Designed as decision support, not prediction: an instrument panel, not a crystal ball.
03 / METHOD

OpenAI scores nearly triple the next closest lab, revealing deep concentration risk at the frontier.

LabScoreFragility

Fragility scores over time

Speaker Notes:
  • Ingest news, filings, and official signals from public sources.
  • Map each event to one of 7 fragility dimensions with a confidence tag.
  • Score labs via binary checklist + confidence-weighted variant.
  • Expose evidence trails, trajectories, and per-lab breakdowns in an open dashboard.
  • The graph animates to show how fragility scores have increased over time as more risk events are indexed.

05 / LIMITATIONS & FUTURE WORK

A transparent, imperfect monitor still outperforms ad hoc intuition when its limitations are visible.

Limitations

  • Selection bias — public reporting overweights visible, English-language firms
  • Subjective coding — even transparent heuristics embed judgment calls
  • No causal guarantee — fragility indicators are warning signs, not deterministic forecasts

Future work

  • Inter-rater reliability — crowdsource coding to reduce single-coder bias
  • Non-English sources — expand coverage to Chinese, European, and Korean labs
  • Causal modeling — test whether fragility scores correlate with real incidents
  • API access — expose data as a public API for downstream research tools
Speaker Notes:
  • Selection bias: public reporting overweights visible, English-language firms.
  • Subjective coding: even transparent heuristics embed judgment calls.
  • No causal guarantee: fragility indicators are warning signs, not deterministic forecasts.
  • A transparent, imperfect monitor still outperforms ad hoc intuition — especially when its limitations are visible and its evidence is open.
